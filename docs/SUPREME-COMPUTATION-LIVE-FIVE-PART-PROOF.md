## Why this matters

Most software asks:

**Can this instruction run?**

Security usually asks:

**Does this identity have permission?**

Monitoring asks:

**What happened after it ran?**

Supreme Computation joins those questions into one governed chain:

**What is being attempted? What is true right now? What evidence supports the change? Who has authority? What does the action refer to? What caused it? Who is accountable? If the transition is admissible, execute it. Then observe what actually happened, verify the consequence, create a receipt, and carry the verified result forward as the next state.**

In plain English:

> **The machine does not get to change the system just because it can. The change has to prove it belongs there, and the system has to prove what happened afterward.**
