License Supreme Computation and SCQOS

What a Company Is Paying For

The public repository shows how Supreme Computation and SCQOS work.

A company pays to connect that architecture to its real business, using its systems, rules, information, people, authority, risks, and responsibilities.

Before the company’s AI or software performs an important action, SCQOS checks whether the complete action makes sense and is allowed right now.

SCQOS returns one of three decisions:

PERMIT means the action proved itself and may continue.

HOLD means something is missing, outdated, uncertain, or contradictory. The action must wait.

REJECT means the action violates a required rule or boundary and must stop.

If an action is permitted, SCQOS records what actually happened and creates a tamper-evident digital receipt connecting the request, evidence, rules, decision, action, and result.

What Can Be Licensed

A commercial agreement can include:

* Connecting SCQOS to a company’s AI, application, API, cloud, Kubernetes, Linux, classical, hybrid, or quantum system.
* Creating company-specific rules, boundaries, authority, and evidence requirements.
* Operating SCQOS as a managed service.
* Installing SCQOS inside the company’s own environment.
* Creating private adapters and components.
* Generating, storing, and verifying cryptographic receipts.
* Testing old information, wrong identities, contradictions, missing authority, broken verification, and other failures before launch.
* Maintenance, updates, training, support, and service commitments.

The public repository shows the engine.

Commercial licensing turns that engine into a fitted, tested, supported control system for a real organization.

What Remains Public

Code already released under the MIT License remains available under that license.

A company is not paying for permission it already has under the MIT License. It is paying for the private integration, configuration, operation, testing, verification, support, and responsibility required to make SCQOS work inside its actual environment.

How a Company Can Start

A company does not need to change its entire operation at once.

The first pilot can govern one important action, such as:

* An AI issuing a refund.
* An agent sending customer information.
* Software changing a financial record.
* A cloud system deploying infrastructure.
* Kubernetes admitting a workload.
* A sensitive computer process starting.

For that action, the company and SCQOS define:

1. What must be checked.
2. What evidence counts.
3. Who has authority.
4. Which boundaries cannot be crossed.
5. What should produce PERMIT, HOLD, or REJECT.
6. What proof must be saved.
7. How the real result will be confirmed.

When the Pilot Is Successful

The pilot succeeds only when it proves that:

* The action is caught before it happens.
* Correct actions can receive PERMIT.
* Incomplete or uncertain actions receive HOLD.
* Prohibited actions receive REJECT.
* Missing evidence or broken verification never silently becomes permission.
* The real result is connected to the original decision.
* The company or an independent reviewer can verify the receipt.
